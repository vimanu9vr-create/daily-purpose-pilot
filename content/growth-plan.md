# If this were my app

What I'd do, in order, and why. Opinionated on purpose — you can argue with any
of it, but the worst option is doing a bit of everything.

---

## The thing to accept first

Nobody is looking for a manifestation app. The category is full, the App Store
is full, and "manifestation app" as a search term brings you competitors with
years of reviews.

What people *are* doing is scrolling, feeling stuck at 11pm, and occasionally
finding something that describes their situation better than they can. That is
the only door in. So the account isn't marketing for the app — the account is the
product people meet first, and the app is what happens to the ones who stay.

That reframe changes every decision below.

---

## 1. Stop being a manifestation account

A manifestation account competes with tens of thousands. An account for people
who *can't say affirmations with a straight face* competes with almost nobody,
and it's the same audience minus the people who were never going to buy anything.

Position it out loud, in the bio, in the first line of every caption:

> Manifestation for people who feel ridiculous saying "I am abundant."

This is not a slogan. It's a filter. It repels the crowd that wants the universe
to handle it — who were never going to pay you — and it magnetises the people who
have tried this, failed, felt stupid, and quietly still want it to work. That
second group is your entire business, and right now nobody is talking to them.

The research supports it: the documented complaint in these communities is that
affirming what you don't believe "feels like lying to yourself", and there's an
active backlash against manifestation's toxic positivity. That grievance has an
audience and no address. Be the address.

---

## 2. One format, not ten

The instinct is variety. The instinct is wrong at this stage.

Pick the swap format — ~~generic affirmation~~ → absurdly specific one — and run
it into the ground. Reasons:

**It's infinitely repeatable.** Money, love, career, confidence, health, moving
country, leaving a job, getting out of debt. That's fifty videos without
repeating yourself.

**Every single one demonstrates the app.** The app's job is writing the specific
line. The format's job is proving the specific line is better. They're the same
argument, which means you're never choosing between growing and selling.

**Repetition is how a stranger learns what you are.** If someone sees three of
your videos in a fortnight and they're three different formats, you're noise. If
they're three swaps, you're *the person who rewrites people's affirmations* — and
that's a thing they can describe to a friend, which is what a send is.

Vary the subject, never the shape.

---

## 3. Post twice a day for thirty days, then look

Not because volume is magic. Because you need enough data points to find the one
that works, and with a new account most posts reach almost nobody regardless of
quality. Sixty posts gives you maybe two or three that break out. Ten posts gives
you nothing to learn from.

Set the bar at "good enough to send", not "perfect". The pipeline we've built can
produce one of these in minutes.

**Only two numbers matter for the first month:** sends per view, and average
watch time as a percentage of length. Sends carry three to five times the weight
of likes for reaching non-followers — which is everyone, for you. Views are
vanity right now; ignore them completely.

---

## 4. Spend thirty minutes a day in other people's comments

This is the most underrated thing available to you and it costs nothing.

The audience is already assembled. It's sitting in the comment sections of the
big manifestation accounts, right now, tonight. You don't need the algorithm's
permission to reach it.

But don't leave "great post 🙌". Do the thing the app does, by hand, in public:
find someone who's written their goal in a comment and reply with the specific
affirmation you'd write for them. One line, free, no pitch.

That is a product demo, a proof of expertise and an act of generosity in the same
sentence. People click the profile of someone who just did that for them. Do it
twenty times a day and it will out-perform your first month of posting.

It's also how you get your first testimonials for the workbook, which you still
don't have.

---

## 5. Collect emails, not followers

Followers are rented. The platform decides who sees you, and it changes its mind.
Email is yours.

The free Day One sample already exists and already captures an address through
Lemon Squeezy. So the bio link goes to the **workbook page**, not the app —
because a stranger understands a sales page and doesn't understand a signup
screen. The free sample catches the ones who won't pay today, and the app upsell
already sits on the thank-you page for the ones who do.

One link, one funnel, in that order. Don't put four links in your bio.

Then actually email those people. Once a week, one idea, no hard sell.

---

## 6. Do not run ads yet

You'll be tempted, because ads feel like doing something and organic feels like
shouting into a well.

But paid amplifies a signal you don't have yet. You don't know which hook works,
which audience responds, or what your app's retention looks like — so you'd be
paying to learn things a month of free posting will tell you.

The trigger to start: one organic video clears roughly 50,000 views without help.
Then put money behind *that exact creative*, unchanged. Not a new ad. The one
that already proved strangers respond to it.

---

## 7. Fix the leak before you pour

Every visitor you send to a leaky app is money set on fire, and this is the part
founders skip because it's less fun than making content.

Before the traffic arrives, know: what percentage of people who open the app type
a goal? Of those, how many come back on day two? Day seven? If day-two retention
is under 20%, more traffic makes the problem more expensive, not smaller.

You have the data to answer this. Nobody has looked at it.

---

## 8. The unglamorous ones that matter anyway

**Buy a domain.** `workers.dev` reads as a test deployment to anyone who looks.
Ten dollars fixes your checkout branding, your bio link and your email
authentication at once.

**Finish the Play listing.** Twelve testers, fourteen days. That clock hasn't
started, and it's pure waiting time you could have running in the background.

**Get three real testimonials.** The sales page has the section built and empty.
Ten free copies to people who fit the audience, then one question: *what did you
notice?*

---

## What this looks like as a week

Two videos a day from the swap format, same shape, different subject. Thirty
minutes a day writing real affirmations for strangers in other people's comment
sections. One email a week to whoever the free sample has collected. Every
Sunday, read sends per view and watch time, keep the shape of whatever won, throw
the rest away.

That's it. No new funnel, no new platform, no rebuild.

---

## The honest part

The first three weeks will look like nothing is happening. Almost all of it is,
in fact, nothing happening — that's the normal shape of this, not a sign the plan
is wrong.

The failure mode isn't picking the wrong strategy. It's changing strategy in
week two because week one was quiet, and thereby never accumulating enough of
anything to learn from.

Pick this, or pick something else, and then don't move for thirty days.
