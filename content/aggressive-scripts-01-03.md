# Three aggressive sub-10s scripts — workbook traffic

Faceless. Shot on a phone, not rendered. Destination: the Seven-Day Reset page
via link in bio. Never show a URL on screen.

---

## The strategy note you should read before filming

**The brief has one internal conflict and it's worth naming.** Link clicks are
not a ranking signal. Watch time and sends are. An ending that pushes people off
Instagram is the weakest possible ending for distribution — and at your current
reach the click trigger is converting somewhere around a twentieth of a person
per post. The bottleneck is not the CTA. It is that almost nobody sees these.

So each script below is built to survive on watch time first. The click trigger
is there because you asked for it and because it costs about 1.5 seconds. **Once
you're testing purely for reach, cut beat 4 and let the video end on the turn** —
you'll get a tighter loop and a stronger completion rate, and the people who want
the workbook will find the bio anyway.

**Why these are filmed and not rendered.** Days 1–5 were typographic and got 8
views. Raw is currently outperforming polished, and a real hand tearing real
paper under real light is a pattern interrupt in a way that a fade-in can never
be. Each of these needs one prop and under thirty seconds of filming. Shoot
top-down on a plain surface, daylight from one side, phone on a stack of books.

**Every claim below is true and checkable against the workbook.** The mechanism
is attention and follow-through — you notice what you've given your attention a
shape to match, and you act sooner on something you've rehearsed. Nothing here
promises an outcome, and nothing invents a result.

**All three loop.** The last frame returns to the opening prop so the cut back is
invisible. Rewatches count as watch time, which at under nine seconds is most of
your distribution.

## SCRIPT 1 — "The argument you always lose"

**Total: 8.5s**

### 1 · Scroll-stopper visual correction (0–1.5s)

Top-down. A hand already mid-motion at frame 0 — writing **"I am a
millionaire"** in marker on white paper, fast and slightly messy. At 1.2s the
hand grabs the sheet and **rips it in half through the middle of the word
"millionaire"**, hard enough that the halves jump.

No cut. One take. The tear is the interrupt, and it has to be audible even
though the audio will be replaced — the *look* of a violent tear reads on mute.

### 2 · The shock hook (text on screen, bold, centre)

> **Your bank app disagrees.**
> **That's why it doesn't work.**

Lands at 0.3s, over the writing, before the tear. Confrontational but not
insulting — it accuses the method, not the viewer, which is the line between
provocative and unfollowable.

### 3 · The speed body (1.5–6s) — 33 words

> You can't affirm past something you can see is false. Your mind argues and
> wins. The Seven-Day Reset drops the lying — seven pages that rebuild what you
> notice and how fast you move.

On screen, three staggered lines over the torn halves:

- 1.8s **You can't argue yourself into believing it.**
- 3.4s **You always lose that argument.**
- 5.0s **So stop having it.**

### 4 · The click trigger (6–8s)

Hands push the two torn halves aside; underneath is a clean page with one
handwritten line already on it.

> **Seven days. Ten minutes each. £29 — link in bio.**

*(Set the currency to whatever Lemon Squeezy is actually charging. Don't guess
on screen.)*

### 5 · Loop (8–8.5s)

Cut back to the marker touching blank paper — the opening frame. The loop reads
as "start again, properly."

### Targeting metadata

`manifestation not working` · `affirmations that actually work` ·
`law of attraction reality check` · `manifestation journal prompts` ·
`self concept work`

## SCRIPT 2 — "Three years"

**Total: 9s**

### 1 · Scroll-stopper visual correction (0–1.5s)

A stack of five or six self-help and manifestation paperbacks **dropped flat
onto a hard desk from about 30cm**, in frame at 0.0s already falling. Real thud,
slight bounce, one book slides off the top.

Film it twice and keep the messier take. The imperfection is the point.

### 2 · The shock hook

> **Three years of this and you**
> **still can't say what you want.**

At 0.2s, over the falling stack. This is the most confrontational of the three
and it's the one most likely to produce comments, because a large number of
people will recognise themselves and a smaller number will want to argue.

Both are conversation, which is a ranked signal. Reply to every one.

### 3 · The speed body (1.5–6s) — 26 words

> Vagueness is protection. If you never name it, you never fail at it. Day one
> is one sentence, ten minutes, and it's the one nobody writes.

On screen:

- 1.9s **"Abundance" is not a goal.**
- 3.5s **It's a way of not saying the real one.**
- 5.1s **Because naming it means you could miss.**

### 4 · The click trigger (6–8s)

The books are swept out of frame by a forearm. One thin page is placed down in
the empty space.

> **Day one is one sentence. Link in bio.**

### 5 · Loop (8–9s)

Hand lifts the page away, revealing the desk — then cut to the stack falling
again. It reads as a cycle the viewer is being invited to break.

### Targeting metadata

`manifestation for beginners` · `how to set goals that stick` ·
`why manifesting isn't working` · `self improvement books` ·
`manifestation workbook`

## SCRIPT 3 — "Mood versus plan"

**Total: 8s**

### 1 · Scroll-stopper visual correction (0–1.5s)

Hard vertical split screen, both halves moving at frame 0.

**Left:** a phone screen scrolling fast through a saved Pinterest board —
kitchens, cars, beaches, gym mirrors. Blur it slightly; it must read as generic,
not aspirational.

**Right:** a hand writing a single line on paper, in real time, so the words
appear as the viewer watches.

The mismatch between the two speeds is the interrupt — frantic against slow.

### 2 · The shock hook

> **One of these is a mood.**
> **One of these is a plan.**

At 0.25s, straddling the split. Not an insult, a sorting question — and sorting
questions are unusually send-able, because the viewer immediately thinks of
someone who is on the wrong side of it.

### 3 · The speed body (1.5–6s) — 26 words

> Pictures of other people's kitchens do nothing. A scene detailed enough that
> your mind stops filing it as fiction changes what you notice. That's day two.

On screen, on the right half only:

- 2.0s **Not the keys. The Tuesday.**
- 3.6s **Not the house. The light in it at 4pm.**
- 5.2s **Detail is what makes your mind accept it.**

### 4 · The click trigger (6–7s)

Left half goes black. The right half widens to full frame, the handwritten line
alone.

> **Seven pages. Link in bio.**

### 5 · Loop (7–8s)

The page is lifted, revealing the scrolling phone underneath — back to frame 0.
The board was always under it.

### Targeting metadata

`vision board alternative` · `visualisation technique` ·
`manifestation that works` · `how to actually manifest` ·
`intentional living 2026`

## How to run these

**Post one per day, not all three.** Three variables at once tells you nothing.
Script 2 first — the book drop is the strongest physical interrupt and "three
years" is the most self-recognising hook of the three.

**Read two numbers only.** Average watch time as a percentage of length, and
sends. Ignore views entirely for now; at this volume views measure the size of
the test pool, not the quality of the video.

**If watch time is strong and sends are zero**, the ending is the problem — not
the hook. Sharpen the last line before you touch anything else.

**If watch time is weak**, the interrupt didn't connect to the body. That's the
documented failure mode of pattern-interrupt hooks: highest one-second retention
of any family, worst completion when the shock doesn't pay off.

**Audio:** trending track from the Reels library, added in the app. Mood is
blunt and percussive rather than dreamy for these three — they're arguments, not
meditations. No swell needed; they're too short.

**Comment prompt for all three, pinned by you:**
*"Tell me the goal you've been keeping vague and I'll write the specific
version."* Every reply is a free public demo of exactly what you sell.
