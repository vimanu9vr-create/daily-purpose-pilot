# Affiliate outreach

Turn on affiliates in Lemon Squeezy first — Settings » Affiliates → enable,
commission **40%**, applied to The Seven-Day Reset. About $11.60 to them and
roughly $15 net to you per sale, costing nothing unless it works.

**How joining actually works.** Affiliates always apply to you — you cannot add
anyone manually. They find your programme either from the Program list in their
Affiliate Hub (passive, a trickle, don't rely on it) or from the affiliate signup
URL on your Settings » Affiliates page, which is the one you share.

Lemon Squeezy vets every affiliate itself before they can apply to any programme,
so there's an approval step on their side before yours. Tell people that up front
or half your yeses will stall on a step they weren't expecting.

Keep **auto-approval off**, at least at first, so you see who's asking and can
read their introduction. Note that rejecting someone is close to permanent — they
can't reapply without Lemon Squeezy support intervening — so ignore rather than
reject anyone you're unsure about.

---

## Who to message

Not the biggest accounts. The ones at **5,000–50,000 followers** who post
consistently and have **nothing of their own to sell**. They're the sweet spot:
big enough to matter, small enough to reply, and actively looking for a way to
earn from an audience they've already built.

Signals worth having: they post about journaling or scripting rather than just
quotes, their comment sections have real conversations rather than emoji, and
their bio has no product link.

Avoid anyone already running their own course or workbook — you're competition,
not an opportunity.

Do not message fifty in one day. Ten a day, each with a genuinely specific first
line, or Instagram will treat you as spam and you'll lose the account you haven't
built yet.

**Warm them first.** Leave real comments on their posts for a few days before you
DM. Not "love this 🙌" — actually write someone a specific affirmation in their
comment section. When the DM arrives you're a name they've seen being useful,
which is the entire difference between a reply and a delete.

---

## The DM

Short deliberately. No link in the first message — a link from a new account with
no history is the fastest way into a spam folder, and the goal here is a reply,
not a click.

> Hi [name] — I've been in your comments the last week or so, the [specific
> thing they posted about] one especially.
>
> I've written a seven-day manifestation workbook. It's the honest version —
> it opens by saying that thinking about something doesn't make it arrive, and
> then gives people the actual questions.
>
> I'm setting up an affiliate programme at 40%. Before I pitch you on anything,
> would you just want a free copy to look at? If you think it's rubbish, tell me
> and I'll go away. If you like it, we can talk.
>
> Either way, no obligation.

Why it works: it opens with proof you're not a bot, the product is described in
one line with a real position rather than adjectives, the commission is stated
immediately so they're not reading a mystery, and the ask is "look at this" not
"promote this" — which is almost impossible to say no to.

The line about telling you it's rubbish matters. It signals you can hear no, which
makes people far more willing to say yes.

## If they reply yes

Send the PDF directly. Not a link to buy it — the actual file, free, no gate.

> Here it is — yours, no strings.
>
> If it's useful, here's the signup link for the affiliate programme — 40% per
> sale, about $11.60: [your affiliate signup URL]
>
> Two things worth knowing so it isn't confusing: Lemon Squeezy vets affiliates
> themselves before you can apply to any programme, so there's one approval step
> on their side first. Then you apply to mine and I'll accept you straight away.
>
> One thing that would genuinely help either way: if you do Day 1 and Day 2, I'd
> love to know what you noticed. I'm still collecting honest feedback and I'd
> rather have yours than a nice review.

That last paragraph is doing double duty. Whether or not they become an affiliate,
you may get the testimonial the sales page is still missing.

## Follow-up, once, after five days

> No worries if it's not for you — just closing the loop so I'm not sitting here
> wondering. Happy to leave you alone either way.

One follow-up only. Never a second.

## What to expect

Ten messages might get two or three replies and perhaps one active affiliate.
That's a normal rate, not a failure. Twenty active affiliates is a business;
getting there takes a couple of hundred conversations over a few months.

The compounding part: every one of these conversations is also a potential
testimonial, a potential bundle partner, and someone who now knows your name in a
niche where almost nobody does.

# Affiliate Hub copy

What people see when they land on your Lemon Squeezy affiliate page. This sells
the *opportunity*, not the workbook — different audience, different job.

An affiliate's real fear isn't a low commission. It's recommending something that
makes them look bad to an audience they spent years building. So the copy leads
with why it won't embarrass them.

## Programme name

`The Seven-Day Reset — Affiliate Programme`
