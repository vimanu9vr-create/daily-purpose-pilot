# Ten reels for people who have never heard of you

Built for a cold feed. Nobody here knows your name, so nothing assumes they do —
no "as I always say", no callbacks, no in-jokes. Each one has to earn its own
first two seconds from zero.

Ratio across the ten: six emotional, two educational, two product. Post in the
order given at the end, not the order written here.

Everything obeys the rules your reel engine already runs on — real app features
only, no invented testimonials or user numbers, no guaranteed outcomes, no URL
ever on screen, palette CREAM / ROSE / BURG / PLUM / MUTE, Lora for emphasis and
Poppins Light for body, 15–20s unless noted, motion in frame 0.

A note before the scripts. Your bio link now has two possible destinations — the
app and the $29 workbook. Send cold traffic to the **workbook** page. It explains
itself to a stranger in a way the app's signup screen can't, and it has the app
upsell built into the thank-you page. One link, in that order.

## V1 · The drafts folder

**Primary emotion** Longing, then relief
**Pillar** Emotional / relatable · **Length** 18s

**Hook (0–2s)** "You typed the whole thing out. Then you deleted it."

**Voiceover**
> You typed the whole thing out. Then you deleted it.
> Not because you didn't mean it. Because saying it out loud makes it real, and
> real is the part you're not ready for.
> So it sits there. Unsent. For months.
> Tonight, don't send it to them. Write it down for you. One sentence. The true
> one, not the tidy one.

**On-screen text** (one to two lines per beat)
`You typed it out.` → `Then you deleted it.` → `Not because you didn't mean it.`
→ `Because real is the part you're not ready for.` → `Write it down tonight.`
→ `The true one. Not the tidy one.`

**Scene by scene**
- 0–2s · Phone screen in the dark, thumb hovering, a text cursor blinking at the
  end of a long paragraph. The paragraph is blurred past legibility.
- 2–4s · Text deletes itself backwards, character by character, fast. This is the
  motion that holds the first beat.
- 4–9s · Face lit only by the phone, eyes down. Slow push in. No dialogue.
- 9–13s · Phone goes face down on the duvet. Screen light dies. Room goes dark.
- 13–18s · Bedside lamp clicks on. A hand, a cheap notebook, a pen. Writing
  begins but we never read the words.

**AI video-generation prompt**
> Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, cinematic short-film look. A person in their late
> twenties lies in bed in a dark room at night, illuminated only by the cold blue
> light of a phone held close to their face. Shallow depth of field, 50mm lens,
> f/1.8, soft natural falloff into black. Their expression is tired and
> conflicted — not crying, just very still. Slow 10% push-in over six seconds.
> Then the phone is placed screen-down on a duvet and the blue light vanishes,
> leaving near-total darkness for one beat. A warm bedside lamp clicks on from
> frame left, motivated practical light, tungsten 2700K. Hands open a plain
> notebook on the bedcovers and begin writing with a pen; we see the hands and
> the page edge but never the words. Muted desaturated palette, deep burgundy and
> cream tones, fine 35mm grain. Handheld with almost imperceptible drift. No text
> overlays in the generation — text added in post, centred, between y=690 and
> y=1450.

**Music mood** Sparse piano, single sustained note under the deletion, warmth
entering exactly when the lamp clicks on at 13s.

**Pacing** Slow. Let the dark beat at 12–13s sit a full second. The silence is
the retention device.

**Caption**
> The message you keep rewriting isn't really for them. It's the first honest
> sentence you've written about your own life in months. Write that one instead.

**CTA** "What's the sentence you haven't said out loud? One word is enough."

**Product integration** None on screen. This one exists to be sent to someone.

# Post these three first

**1 · V4 — Text from a year out.**
It's the highest send-rate concept in the set, and sends are what a cold account
needs most: Instagram treats a send as a much stronger signal than a like, and a
send puts you in front of someone who has a reason to look at your profile. The
format also carries zero brand knowledge — a stranger doesn't need to know who
you are for a message thread to work. And the typing indicator between bubbles
keeps motion alive during a slow read, which is usually where these die.

**2 · V3 — You're not lazy.**
The strongest first two seconds in the set, because it contradicts something the
viewer believes about themselves. Contradiction beats agreement for scroll-stop
every time. It's also the most quotable, which is what gets it into other
people's stories. The two seconds of silence under the hook will feel wrong when
you edit it — leave it.

**3 · V6 — You've been visualising the wrong thing.**
Post this third because by then you need a reason for someone to follow rather
than just watch. The first two make people feel understood; this one proves you
know something they don't. It's also the safest claim in the set — attention and
follow-through, nothing supernatural — and it points naturally at the workbook
without selling.

Hold V9 and V10 until you've posted at least six. Selling to an account with no
history converts badly, and the two product videos are more valuable once a few
hundred people have already decided they like you.

## What to watch

Ignore views. For the first two weeks the only numbers worth reading are **sends
per view** and **average watch time as a percentage of length**. A 15-second
video held to 90% is worth more than a 30-second video held to 40%, and the
algorithm agrees.

If V1–V6 get watched but not sent, the endings are too neutral — the fix is a
sharper final line, not a different hook. If they get sent but not followed, the
problem is your profile grid, not the videos.
