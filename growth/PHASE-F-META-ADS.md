# Phase F — Meta / Instagram ads

## A correction before anything else

I researched Meta's current policy before writing these, and **the ad copy I gave you in Phase 1 would likely have been rejected.**

Meta's personal attributes policy prohibits ad copy that implies or asserts knowledge of a person's health status, mental state or personal struggles. Second-person constructions — "you have", "are you struggling with", "do you find it hard to" — are specifically called out. March 2026 brought a significant escalation in enforcement, with multimodal AI detection that is faster and much more precise than the old keyword matching.

Three of my five earlier ads open in exactly the banned register:

- ~~"You're not undisciplined."~~ — asserts a personal trait
- ~~"You didn't fail at manifesting."~~ — asserts a personal history
- ~~"Your vision board isn't the problem."~~ — assumes they own one and that something is wrong

The fix is not to soften the idea. It's to **move it into the third person and let the reader recognise themselves**, which is more persuasive anyway — being told who you are provokes resistance, while watching someone describe a person who happens to be you does not.

> ❌ "You quit on day four."
> ✅ "Most people quit on day four."

Every ad below is written in that register. Read your own final copy against this rule before publishing, and check the policy yourself — it changes, and this note will age.

## Campaign structure

```
Campaign: ManifestAI — Beta Recruitment
  Objective:  Sales / Leads  (NOT Traffic, NOT Engagement)
  Conversion: "Beta Application (Android)" custom conversion  ← from Phase D
  Budget:     ₹500/day, campaign-level (Advantage CBO), 7 days
  
  Ad Set A — Broad          60% of delivery
  Ad Set B — Interest       40% of delivery
      5 ads each, same creative set
```

### Ad Set A — Broad
| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Location | India |
| Age | 24–34 |
| Gender | Women |
| Detailed targeting | **None.** At this budget Meta's algorithm outperforms interest stacks |
| Devices | **Android only** — you cannot test on iOS, so an iPhone click is money burned |
| Placements | Advantage+, then **exclude Audience Network** (cheap junk clicks) |
| Optimisation | Conversions → Beta Application (Android) |

### Ad Set B — Interest
Same as above, plus: Law of Attraction, Manifestation, Affirmations, Personal development, Journaling, Meditation, Mindfulness, *The Secret*.

Its real job is to answer one question: does the category label pull harder than the problem statement? That tells you which way to write launch copy later.

**Gender targeting justification:** the app's own language, visual identity and existing dream data skew heavily female. This is an editorial fit judgement, not an assumption about who manifests. If Ad Set A underdelivers, open it to all genders before you touch anything else — broadening is cheaper than new creative.

### Budget

| | |
|---|---|
| Daily | ₹500 |
| Test period | 7 days |
| Total | **₹3,500** |
| Expected CPM (India) | ₹80–200 |
| Expected CTR | 1–2.5% |
| Expected landing conversion | 15–30% |
| **Expected cost per qualified applicant** | **₹150–400** |

Combined with personal outreach, that gets you past twenty-five. If you're above ₹600 per applicant after 100 clicks, the problem is the landing page, not the ads — go back to Phase D §6 and diagnose by stage.

**Don't touch the budget for 72 hours.** Every edit resets the learning phase, and at this spend you cannot afford to restart it twice.

## The strongest concept, and why

**Ad 3 — Personalised manifestation.**

Not because it will win on CTR. It probably won't — Ads 1 and 5 will, because problem statements out-click demonstrations almost every time.

Ad 3 wins on the metric that decides this campaign: **cost per retained tester.**

Three reasons.

**It's the only one that proves something.** The other four make claims about how the app is different. Ad 3 shows the difference on screen in fifteen seconds, and personalisation is exactly the claim a sceptical viewer discounts when it's asserted. "I am abundant" beside "I book the window seat for July" does an argument's work without making one.

**It pre-qualifies.** Someone who watches a screen recording and still clicks has understood what they're applying for. Someone who clicks an emotional hook may just be agreeing with a sentiment. Your funnel doesn't need clicks — it needs twelve people who install and stay fourteen days, and that's a much higher bar than interest.

**Your writing is genuinely your best asset.** From the product teardown: *"I book the window seat for July and close the tab."* That's better copy than most of this category's marketing, and it's sitting inside the product being seen by nobody. Ad 3 is the only concept that puts it in front of people.

**Judge it on cost per qualified applicant, not CTR.** If you optimise this campaign on CTR you will kill Ad 3 on day two and be left with ads that produce enthusiasm instead of testers.

## Creative testing plan

**Days 1–3.** All five ads live in both ad sets. Change nothing. Meta needs the window, and you need clean data more than you need speed.

**Day 3.** Kill any ad under 1% CTR after 2,000 impressions. Kill any ad with zero applications after 50 clicks — that's a landing-page mismatch specific to that ad's promise.

**Day 4.** Move budget to the top two by **cost per qualified applicant**. Write three variations of the winning *angle* — different opening line, different visual, same underlying idea. Do not introduce a sixth new angle; you'd be restarting the test with less budget.

**Day 7.** Decide. Either you have enough applicants and you stop, or one angle is clearly working and you extend it at the same daily budget. Do not raise the budget to fix a bad result — spending more on an ad that isn't converting just buys the same failure faster.

**Kill criteria, in order:** no applications after 50 clicks → CTR under 1% at 2,000 impressions → cost per applicant above ₹600 → frequency above 2.5 (you've exhausted the audience).

## Policy checklist before you publish

Run every ad against this. A rejection costs a day; a repeated rejection can cost the ad account.

- [ ] No second-person claims about the reader's state, habits or history
- [ ] No "you struggle with", "you failed", "you're not disciplined"
- [ ] No before/after framing
- [ ] No guaranteed outcomes — no money, love, career or health promises
- [ ] No implication that thinking causes events
- [ ] No health or medical claims of any kind, including about the audio
- [ ] No fake scarcity — "20 places" is true, keep it true
- [ ] No asking for reviews anywhere in the funnel
- [ ] Landing page claims match ad claims
- [ ] Domain verified in Business Settings

Sources for the policy points are in the chat message accompanying this file. Verify against Meta's own Advertising Standards before spending — the guidance above reflects what's published now, and enforcement moved sharply in March 2026.
