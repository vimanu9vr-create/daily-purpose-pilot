# ManifestAI — 9 ad scripts

Three hooks, three variants each. Every one runs 20–24 seconds, ends naming
ManifestAI, and uses its own affirmation set so you can run all nine
concurrently without the algorithm — or a viewer — seeing the same reel twice.

**Format for all nine:** 9:16, talking head or text-on-screen, one phrase per
caption card, trending audio added in the Instagram app (not desktop).

---

## A note on the copyright question, since it's the reason you asked

The affirmations below are original. That is not me being precious — it is the
safer choice, and here is the honest reasoning.

Lightly editing someone else's lines is the worst of both worlds. It stays close
enough that a side-by-side comparison still reads as derivative, and it loses
the thing that made the original land. "Small changes" is the version most
likely to get you both a complaint *and* a weaker ad.

What you genuinely cannot own, and what nobody can claim against you: the
format (talking head, one-phrase captions, affirmations read aloud, cut to app),
the hook phrasing (these are search queries, not creative work — "how to
manifest millions" is typed into TikTok thousands of times a day), and the
underlying idea that specific affirmations beat vague ones.

What you can be claimed against: her exact sentences in her exact order. So
none of that is here.

**The bigger risk is not copyright, it's ad policy.** Meta and TikTok both
reject ads that promise financial outcomes. "Manifest millions" as organic
content is fine; the same words as a *paid* ad can be refused under misleading
claims. Every script below therefore promises a **practice** — writing,
rehearsing, repeating — and never an outcome. Scripts 3, 6 and 9 are the
softest on this and are your safest bets if an account gets flagged.

# HOOK 1 — "How to manifest millions."

## Script 1 — The car

> How to manifest millions.
>
> Not by saying "I am a millionaire."
>
> You've said that one in the car. Nothing moved.
>
> Say something you could actually picture happening.
>
> "I check the price last, not first."
>
> "I book the flight the same day I decide to go."
>
> "Money leaves and it comes back bigger."
>
> Then say it again tomorrow. That's the part everybody skips.
>
> ManifestAI writes yours. Then twenty-one days of five minutes.

**Why it works:** the reversal lands three seconds in, so the viewer who
stopped for "millions" is immediately told something they didn't expect.
"In the car" is the recognition line — it's where people actually say
affirmations, and nobody reflects it back at them.

---

## Script 2 — The fourth morning

> How to manifest millions.
>
> You've tried. You lasted four mornings.
>
> That's not discipline. That's the sentence being too big to believe.
>
> Shrink it until you can see it:
>
> "I pay the bill without opening the app first."
>
> "I say yes before I check the balance."
>
> "The number went up while I was asleep."
>
> A sentence you believe gets repeated. That's the whole mechanism.
>
> ManifestAI writes yours.

**Why it works:** it removes blame. Most people in this audience privately
think they failed because they weren't disciplined enough. Telling them it was
the sentence, not them, is the most persuasive thing you can say.

---

## Script 3 — The test

> How to manifest millions.
>
> One question tells you if your affirmation will work.
>
> Could you picture it happening this year?
>
> If not, it's a wish. Your mind files it with everything else you don't
> believe.
>
> This passes: "I transfer money to savings and don't feel it."
>
> So does: "I pick the restaurant."
>
> And: "I stopped checking before I tapped."
>
> Small enough to see. That's the only rule.
>
> ManifestAI writes yours.

**Why it works:** it gives away something genuinely useful in fifteen seconds,
which is what earns a save and a share. Also the safest of the three for paid
ads — it promises a test, not a result.

# HOOK 2 — "What's the fastest way to manifest millions?"

## Script 4 — The honest answer

> What's the fastest way to manifest millions?
>
> Repetition. That's it. That's the answer nobody wants.
>
> But it only works if the sentence is believable.
>
> "I am a millionaire" isn't, yet. This is:
>
> "I buy it and the balance barely moves."
>
> "I pay people well and it doesn't hurt."
>
> "The invoice gets paid before I chase it."
>
> Say one of those every morning for three weeks.
>
> ManifestAI writes yours and counts the mornings.

**Why it works:** answering a "fastest" hook with "repetition" is a deliberate
anti-climax, and anti-climax holds attention — the viewer stays to see whether
you're going to justify it.

---

## Script 5 — Why yours didn't work

> What's the fastest way to manifest millions?
>
> Faster than you're doing it now? Change the sentence.
>
> Yours is too big. Some part of you knows it isn't true, so it gets ignored.
>
> Try the version you can see:
>
> "I take the earlier flight."
>
> "I give without checking what's left."
>
> "My account looks the same after I pay."
>
> You rehearse those for a week and something shifts. Not magic. Attention.
>
> ManifestAI writes yours.

**Why it works:** "not magic, attention" is a credibility line. This audience
is more sceptical than it looks, and naming the mechanism makes you the one
account that isn't overpromising.

---

## Script 6 — Five minutes

> What's the fastest way to manifest millions?
>
> Five minutes, every morning, on the same three sentences.
>
> Not twenty minutes once. Not a journal you abandon by Thursday.
>
> Three sentences you can picture:
>
> "I round up when I tip."
>
> "I replace it instead of repairing it."
>
> "I said the number without flinching."
>
> Boring. Repeatable. That's why it works.
>
> ManifestAI writes yours and reminds you.

**Why it works:** it sells the *app's actual feature* — the morning reminder —
rather than a vibe. "Boring, repeatable" is a differentiator in a feed built
entirely on aspiration.

# HOOK 3 — "What's the easiest way to manifest?"

## Script 7 — Lower the bar

> What's the easiest way to manifest?
>
> Lower the sentence until it stops feeling like a lie.
>
> Everyone starts at "I am wealthy" and quits. Start here:
>
> "I bought the good one."
>
> "I didn't ask the price."
>
> "I paid it today, not Friday."
>
> Easy to believe means easy to repeat. Easy to repeat is the only thing that
> works.
>
> ManifestAI writes yours.

**Why it works:** "stops feeling like a lie" names the exact sensation of
saying an affirmation you don't believe. Specific internal experiences are what
make a stranger feel understood.

---

## Script 8 — The dusty glass

> What's the easiest way to manifest?
>
> Empty the glass before you pour.
>
> Affirmations are clean water. If the glass is still dusty, it doesn't matter
> what you pour in.
>
> So first: write down what you actually believe about money. The real one.
>
> Then replace it with something you can picture:
>
> "I earn it in less time than I used to."
>
> "I kept the money and still said yes."
>
> That order matters. Most people skip straight to pouring.
>
> ManifestAI walks you through both halves.

**Why it works:** the image is memorable and it justifies your seven-day Purge
phase, so it does double duty — this is the one to run against the $79
blueprint, not just the app.

---

## Script 9 — Tomorrow

> What's the easiest way to manifest?
>
> Say it tomorrow as well.
>
> That's the whole thing. Not a longer ritual — the same short one, twice.
>
> Pick a sentence you can see:
>
> "I paid for all of it."
>
> "I kept the better one."
>
> "It arrived and I wasn't surprised."
>
> Then do it again tomorrow. Almost nobody does. That's the advantage.
>
> ManifestAI counts the mornings so you don't have to.

**Why it works:** shortest and softest of the nine, with the lowest claim
density — so this is the one to run first on a cold ad account, and the one to
fall back to if anything gets rejected.

# How to run these

**Rotate by hook, not by script.** Run 1, 4 and 7 first — one from each hook —
so you learn which *question* your audience responds to before you optimise the
body. Testing nine variants of the same hook tells you much less.

**Watch average watch time, not views.** Your 20-second reels were averaging
five seconds, which is a 25% hold. The number to beat is 50%. If a script holds
past the affirmations, the ending is earning its place; if it drops at second
three, the hook and the turn are mismatched.

**The affirmation sets are swappable.** Twenty-seven original lines across nine
scripts. If one set underperforms, move another script's set into that hook
rather than rewriting the structure — the structure is the part that's working.

**Ending shot, all nine:** cut to `ManifestAI_Day14_Demo.mp4` on the line
naming the app. The face builds trust, the screen proves the product exists,
and neither does the job alone.
