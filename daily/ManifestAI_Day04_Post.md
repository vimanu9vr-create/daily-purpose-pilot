## The claim, and whether it holds

The word is **want**.

I checked this rather than asserting it. It's core to Neville Goddard's
self-concept teaching: self-concept is the state you are currently occupying,
and "want" language holds you in wanting rather than in having, which is why he
taught present-tense assumption instead. That's the tradition this audience
already believes in, stated accurately.

The psychology literature supports the narrower framing point from a different
direction — how a statement is phrased materially changes its effect, and
positive affirmations can actively backfire for people with low self-esteem,
which is the same finding as "it has to be believable" arriving by another road.

So the video claims what the tradition teaches and what framing research
supports. It does not claim science proves manifestation.

The three lines:

1. I **want** a job that doesn't drain me.
2. I **want** to stop worrying about money.
3. I **want** someone who chooses me.

They're deliberately ordinary. The reveal only lands if the viewer recognises
their own sentence in at least one of them.

**The fix is included on purpose.** A video that only says "you're doing it
wrong" earns a wince, not a save. Showing the rewrite — *I hand in my notice on a
Friday* — makes it useful, and it's the same specificity argument as Day 03, so
the account is teaching one idea rather than a scatter of tips.

## Caption

There's one word doing more damage to your affirmations than anything else, and
you almost certainly used it today.

"I want a job that doesn't drain me" is not an affirmation. It's an accurate
description of wanting — and if you say it every morning, wanting is the state
you practise. Want is a state, not a step.

The rewrite is always the same move: describe the moment it's already true. Not
"I want a new job" but "I hand in my notice on a Friday."
