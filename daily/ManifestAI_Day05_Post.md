## Research, and what I could and couldn't verify

**Verified.** Mosseri has stated the top three ranking signals are watch time,
likes and sends, and watch time counts rewatches — a reel finished by a
thousand people outranks one abandoned at three seconds by ten thousand. Sends
are the strongest signal specifically for reaching people who don't follow you;
the commonly cited 3–5× multiplier over likes is community-derived, not
confirmed by Meta, and I'm flagging it as such. Distribution starts with a small
test pool and expands only if that pool reacts. Pattern-interrupt hooks hold the
highest one-second retention of the seven hook families, but they collapse if
the interrupt doesn't connect to what follows.

**Verified about the audience.** The documented criticism of vision boards is
not that they're silly — it's that they're pictures of other people's lives,
that they become shopping lists which hold you in wanting, and that they let you
admire fruit from trees you never planted. That is the same complaint as Day
04's *want is a state, not a step*, arriving from a different direction. So this
video continues one argument rather than starting a new one, which is how a
stranger learns what the account is.

**Not verified, and I won't pretend otherwise.** I can't log into Instagram and
can't see the view counts on Days 01–04 — you told me 8 views across two days
and I've reasoned from that number, not from anything I measured. I also can't
tell you which audio is trending today. Use a trending one from the Reels
library that matches the mood described below; I'm not naming a track I haven't
verified.

## What the 8 views actually say

Almost nothing about the videos, and quite a lot about the account.

A month-old account with 11 followers gets a tiny test pool by construction.
Eight views means the pool was shown it and it didn't earn a second one. At that
volume — four posts, maybe thirty impressions total — there is no signal in the
numbers yet. Your own growth plan says sixty posts before there's anything to
learn from.

The one thing worth changing is where the ending spends its ask. Days 03 and 04
both ended on *follow for tomorrow*, which is a promise cashed in by people who
already see your posts. You have eleven of those. **Day 05 ends on a send
instead**, because sends are the only lever that reaches people who don't follow
you, and non-follower reach is the entire problem. That's the CTA rotation for
today and it's not arbitrary.

I'm also dropping the open loop for one day. Two asks in the last two seconds is
one ask too many, and reach beats continuity while nobody is watching.

## Hooks considered

Scored on: readable at frame 0 with sound off · pattern-interrupt strength ·
self-recognition ("this is about me") · does it set up the payoff · does it make
someone want to send it. Ten each.

| Hook | Silent | Interrupt | Recognition | Sets up | Send | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Your vision board is 40 photos of someone else's life.** | 9 | 9 | 10 | 9 | 9 | **46** |
| Your vision board is other people's kitchens. | 9 | 9 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 40 |
| You pinned the holiday. You never pinned the Tuesday. | 8 | 7 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 40 |
| The problem with your vision board isn't the pictures. | 8 | 6 | 8 | 9 | 6 | 37 |
| Delete your vision board. | 9 | 10 | 5 | 4 | 6 | 34 |
| Nobody's vision board has a Tuesday on it. | 7 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 6 | 34 |
| Two minutes beats a vision board. | 7 | 5 | 5 | 7 | 5 | 29 |
| A vision board is a shopping list you're scared to plant. | 5 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 5 | 28 |

**Winner: the first.** "40 photos" is a number, so it reads as something
observed rather than an opinion, and a number is the cheapest way to make a
blunt claim sound like a finding. "Someone else's life" is the grievance the
audience already half-holds, said out loud — which is what makes a person send
it to the friend they made a board with. "Delete your vision board" scores
higher on pure interrupt and I rejected it anyway: it's provocation we don't
follow through on, and the research is explicit that an interrupt which doesn't
connect to the content kills completion.

**First frame:** the board, fifteen tiles, already drifting — and the hook
already fully visible, not fading in. A beat that starts at zero opacity hands a
scrolling stranger an empty cream rectangle to judge, which is the one thing the
first frame must never be.

**The light does the argument.** Cold window wash while the problem is stated,
warm lamp growing from 6.3s as the answer arrives. That arc carries the feeling;
the words only name it.

**It loops.** From 13.3s the board re-forms, so frame 449 is very nearly frame
0 and the cut back is invisible. Rewatches count as watch time, and watch time
is the signal that matters most — a loop is the cheapest retention there is.

## Why this one should work

It's the first video of the five whose ending is aimed at people who don't
follow you. Everything else has been aimed at an audience of eleven.

It also takes the most saturated object in the category — the vision board, the
single most posted manifestation format there is — and turns it into the
problem rather than the content. That's the cheapest attention available,
because the search and the hashtag are already crowded with people posting the
thing this video argues against.

And it's honest. It doesn't say vision boards are stupid or that the app
replaces them. It says the pictures aren't yours, which is true and which the
people who made one in January already quietly suspect.
