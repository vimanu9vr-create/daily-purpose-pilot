## One decision about the hook

An exercise hook has to deliver the exercise. If it promises sixty seconds of
practice and then spends twenty arguing about affirmations, it has lied in the
first frame — the same failure as a line nobody can finish reading, just
further in.

So the beats **are** the exercise, and the saved line is the whole of it in
three instructions. A screenshot of that frame is a usable card, which is the
difference between a video someone agrees with and a video someone keeps.

This is also the first video that gives away something complete. The exercise
is genuinely the workbook's Day 2, in full. That's deliberate: the fastest way
to make someone believe the other six days are worth $29 is to hand them one
that works.

it, three seconds in, with no explanation yet — which is a reason to stay for
the next line rather than a reason to agree and scroll.

enforced by the build. Three short instructions, screenshot-sized. That's the
only frame anyone keeps and it needs time to become a screenshot.

**"That's day one of seven" does the selling without a pitch.** They've just
done one and it took a minute. Six more is an obvious next step rather than an

## Caption

Try this. It takes sixty seconds and it's the exercise most people get wrong.

Say what you want, once, plainly, out loud. Not the polished version you'd
tell someone at a party — the real one.

Then skip the moment you get it. Skip the keys, the offer, the number on the
screen. That part is a highlight reel and your mind files it as fiction,
because it looks like every other fantasy you've had.

Instead, picture an ordinary afternoon months after it's already true. Four
o'clock on a Tuesday. What's the room like. What can you hear. What has
stopped worrying you that used to. Stay there for thirty seconds.

Then name one small thing you could do tonight — small enough that not doing
it would be embarrassing.

*Say it once. Picture the Tuesday. Do one small thing tonight.*

That's it. That's day one of seven, and it's the whole method in miniature:
detail is what stops your mind calling it a daydream, and one small action is
what stops it staying one.
