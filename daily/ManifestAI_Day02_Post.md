## 1. Research

What follows is verified from published sources. I could not log into Instagram
or TikTok, so there are **no view counts here for individual Reels** — I won't
invent them. What I could verify is how the platform ranks, which hook families
retain, and what the audience is currently complaining about. That's enough to
choose a format on.

**Watch time is the #1 ranking signal, and it counts replays.** Instagram
measures total seconds watched rather than 3-second views, so a 15s Reel watched
three times outranks a 60s Reel watched once. This is the single reason today's
video is 15 seconds and loops rather than 20 and ends.

**Sends per reach is the strongest signal for reaching non-followers** — Mosseri
has put it at roughly three to five times the weight of likes. For an account
with no audience, sends are the only lever that exists. Everything below is
built to be sent to one specific person rather than liked by many.

**Pattern-interrupt hooks average 72–84% retention at three seconds**, the
highest of the six hook families; curiosity gap is 65–78%, direct question
58–72%. Instagram also weights the first two seconds harder than TikTok does.
So: a command, not a question, in the first second.

**Loops lift watch time and replays** by closing back to the opening frame.

**Raw is outperforming polished in 2026** — worth knowing, and a genuine risk to
this account's premium look. See the caveat at the bottom.

**The audience's actual complaint is documented and specific.** The recurring
line in manifestation communities is that saying *"I am a millionaire"* while
behind on rent "feels like lying to yourself" and amounts to "gaslighting
yourself" — people build unbelievable affirmations, say them for two days, and
quit. Alongside it there's an active backlash against manifestation's toxic
positivity and the implication that if it hasn't arrived, it's your fault.

**Lucky Girl Syndrome is the saturated lane**, and it's four years old and now
mostly written about critically.

### The opportunity

Everyone is making the positive-affirmation video. The backlash against it is
loud, unserved, and is *exactly* where ManifestAI already sits — "you didn't
fail at manifesting, you ran out of instructions." We're not adapting a trend.
We're taking the counter-position to a saturated one, which is the cheapest
attention there is.

## 2. The concept

**Format adapted:** the swap / "stop doing this, do this" list — evergreen,
fast, screenshot-friendly, and it earns a send because it hands the viewer
something to *show* someone.

**Manifestation adaptation:** each generic affirmation gets struck through and
replaced with an absurdly specific one.

**App connection, not bolted on:** the fourth specific line is the app's own
output. The video sets up a problem the viewer now believes in — *mine has to be
that specific and I don't know how to write it* — and the app is the answer to
that exact question, not a sponsor slate.

**Target emotion:** "I feel seen." Then, immediately: "oh — that's how you do it."

**Audience:** anyone who has said an affirmation in a mirror and felt stupid.
Per the research, that is most of this niche.

---

## 3. Hooks

1. **"Stop saying this."** ← used. Pattern interrupt, three words, a command, readable in under a second with sound off.
2. "Your affirmation isn't working because it isn't true."
3. "'I am abundant' does nothing. Here's what does."
4. "You've been writing affirmations wrong. Not your fault — nobody told you."
5. "Say the embarrassing one instead."

**Why #1 wins:** pattern interrupts test highest at three seconds, Instagram
weights the first two seconds hardest, and a two-word command creates an
obligation to find out *what* — the curiosity gap closes 1.6 seconds later when
the first struck line appears. #3 is the runner-up and is the A/B test.

## 6. Caption

"I am abundant" does nothing while your account says otherwise. That's not you
failing at manifesting — it's your own mind refusing to pretend, which is a
feature.

The line has to be about the actual thing. In your words. Specific enough that
you'd be slightly embarrassed to read it aloud. *"I pay November's rent early
and don't check the balance twice"* does work that *"I am abundant"* cannot.

**CTA:** Link in bio if you want yours written for your goal instead of everyone's.

**Comment prompt:** What's the generic one you've been saying? I'll write you the specific version.

That prompt is deliberate — every answer is a free demonstration of the product,
in public, and replies drive the conversation the algorithm reads as quality.
